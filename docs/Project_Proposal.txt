Project Title: MediVault: An AI-Powered Personal Health Record and Patient–Doctor 
Communication Platform 

Motivation: 
The healthcare system in Bangladesh is often fragmented across doctors, hospitals, diagnostic 
centers, and paper-based records. A patient may visit several doctors over the years, receive 
prescriptions from different providers, complete tests at different diagnostic centers, and keep 
reports as paper documents or photographs on a phone. There is usually no simple way to bring all 
of this information together when it is actually needed. 
This becomes especially difficult when a patient visits a new doctor. The doctor may have only a 
few minutes to understand the patient's condition, while the patient is expected to remember 
previous diagnoses, medications, test results, allergies, and other important details. Even when the 
information exists, finding the right document, understanding it, and comparing it with older 
records can be difficult.

The idea behind MediVault comes from trying to solve this everyday gap. Instead of treating 
medical documents as files that are simply stored, the system aims to turn them into an organized 
and usable health history. Patients can upload their prescriptions and reports, understand and 
search their own records using natural language, and securely share relevant information with 
doctors. Doctors, in turn, can get a clearer view of a patient's previous history without depending 
entirely on memory or scattered paper documents. 
The goal of MediVault is not to replace doctors or make medical decisions, but to make the 
information surrounding a patient's care more organized, accessible, and useful for both patients 
and healthcare professionals in Bangladesh.

Patients will be able to upload or photograph prescriptions, laboratory reports, diagnostic reports, 
and discharge summaries. The documents will go through an OCR and text-processing pipeline to 
extract information such as medications, dosages, diagnoses, test results, and dates. The extracted 
information will be stored in a structured database, while the document text will also be indexed 
for semantic search. Patients will be able to maintain daily health records such as blood glucose, 
blood pressure, weight, and heart rate, and view these values over time. A document-based 
question-answering feature will allow patients to search and ask questions about their own medical 
records in Bangla or English. The system will also include family health management, 
appointment and follow-up reminders, pre-appointment summaries, and periodic health reports. 
Doctors will have role-specific access to patient information that has been shared with them. They 
will be able to review relevant medical history, previous reports, medications, and selected health 
trends during a consultation. Doctors will also be able to create digital prescriptions, which will be 
added to the patient's record. 
Clinic assistants will use the platform to manage appointments and the daily patient queue. They 
will be able to register walk-in patients, assign token numbers, update queue status, and monitor 
waiting times. Queue updates will be reflected in the doctor's interface in real time so that both the 
assistant and doctor have the same current information. 
The platform will use role-based authentication and authorization to control access to patient and 
clinic information. A secure sharing mechanism will allow patients to provide temporary access to 
their records during a consultation. The system will use a relational database for structured medical 
data and a vector database for document retrieval, with a retrieval-based question-answering 
component for queries over patient records. The overall system will focus on organizing, 
retrieving, and sharing existing medical information and will not be used for diagnosis or 
independent medical decision-making.
